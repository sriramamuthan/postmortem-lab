# EP-00 voiceover script: Break it yourself

About 3 minutes at a relaxed pace (~430 words). `[SCREEN]` lines are visual cues, not spoken. `…` means a short pause.

---

## Cold open

June 4th, 1996. Kourou, French Guiana.
Europe’s brand-new rocket, Ariane 5, lifts off on its very first flight.

Thirty-seven seconds later… it breaks apart.
Hundreds of millions of dollars, gone.

Not because of an engine. Not because of a weld.
Because of one number… that didn’t fit in its box.

`[SCREEN] Ariane 5 demo: drag the slider toward 32,767`

This box holds sixteen bits. The biggest number it can store is thirty-two thousand, seven hundred and sixty-seven.

`[SCREEN] Cross the limit, status turns red`

Go one past that… and you’ve just recreated one of the most expensive software bugs in history.

## What this is

`[SCREEN] Site homepage`

I’m Ram, and this is Post-Mortem Lab.

Here’s the idea. Every episode, I take one famous engineering failure and rebuild it.
Not a diagram. Not a slideshow. The actual mechanism, as code you can run in your browser.

You hear the story. You trigger the bug yourself.
Then we fix it, and prove the fix with a test.
And you walk away with one lesson you’ll recognise the next time it shows up in your own work.

## Season 1

`[SCREEN] Roadmap table, scroll slowly`

Here’s season one.

Ariane 5: the number that didn’t fit.
The Mars Climate Orbiter, lost because one team used pounds and the other used newtons.
The Patriot missile at Dhahran, where a clock drifted by a third of a second.
Therac-25, a radiation therapy machine with a race condition.
Knight Capital, which lost four hundred and forty million dollars in forty-five minutes.
Cloudflare, taken down by a single regular expression.
And CrowdStrike: one config file that crashed eight and a half million computers.

## Respect

`[SCREEN] "A note on respect" callout`

Some of these failures cost lives. We’re not here to laugh at anyone.
Every episode is built on the official investigations, and credits the people whose work made these lessons possible.

## How it’s built

`[SCREEN] GitHub repo, then the toolkit tests passing in the terminal`

Everything is open source. Every episode is a tagged release, so you can check out exactly what you see here.

And yes, I build with AI as my pair programmer. I’ll show you how that really goes, including when it gets things wrong.

## Close

`[SCREEN] Back to the Ariane 5 demo, register red`

Episode one rebuilds Ariane 5’s flight software.
Both guidance computers. The code that wasn’t even needed after launch.
And the one line of protection that was left out… to save processor time.

The link’s in the description.
Go break something.

## Recording notes

- Record in a small, soft room (a closet with clothes works). Keep the mic a fist’s width from your mouth.
- Read each section as its own take. Leave 2 seconds of silence between takes for easy cutting.
- Export as WAV or MP3 and run it through Adobe Podcast Enhance (podcast.adobe.com/enhance).
- Save the result as `paramlab.dev/public/audio/ep-00.mp3`. To hear it on the page, add `audio: /audio/ep-00.mp3` to
  the Ep 00 post’s frontmatter.
